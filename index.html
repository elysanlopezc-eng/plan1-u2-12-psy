Total Session Time: 70 Minutes
Tab I • Opening Motivation Estimated Time: 5 Minutes

Opening Marketing Question: Customer Preference

"Imagine our client launched two different energy drinks, but Product A is outperforming Product B by 400%. How could we scientifically find out why customers prefer one product over the other?"

Quick Brainstorming Challenge (Montano Habit: Seek First to Understand)

Click to reveal student response prompts to guide classroom participation:

Intuition/Gut Feeling: Unreliable in business and psychology. Assumptions lead to costly marketing mistakes!
Observational Research: Watching customers buy in real time. Great start, but does it tell us why they feel that way?
Controlled Psychological Experiment: Isolating variables (taste, branding, pricing, packaging) to establish cause and effect.
Key Hook: Psychology provides the rigorous scientific methodology marketing needs to move from guessing to knowing.
Tab II • Individual Task & Guided Concept Time: 20 Minutes (10m Direct Instruction + 10m Individual Practice)

Part 1: Distinguishing Evidence vs. Opinion vs. Assumption

Students review the definitions and classify real marketing statements in their notebooks.

Scientific Evidence

Empirical data gathered through systematic observation or controlled testing.

Opinion

Personal belief, preference, or judgment not backed by empirical proof.

Assumption

A premise taken for granted without verifying facts or testing hypotheses.

Individual Practice Task (Student Notebooks)

Classify each statement below as [E] Evidence, [O] Opinion, or [A] Assumption:

1. "Teenagers buy brand X solely because the packaging is bright green." [ ? ]
2. "Eye-tracking software showed 78% of test subjects looked at the top-right logo first." [ ? ]
3. "I think minimalist website design always looks much better." [ ? ]

Statement 1: [A] Assumption. It assumes cause without controlled data isolating packaging color from price or taste.

Statement 2: [E] Evidence. Quantifiable empirical result measured directly via eye-tracking technology.

Statement 3: [O] Opinion. Reflects subjective aesthetic taste rather than scientific fact.

Tab III • Cooperative Team Challenge Time: 30 Minutes

Marketing Problem Briefing (Team Challenge)

Scenario: "A local artisanal snack company observed a 35% decrease in online repeat purchases among young adults (ages 18-24) over the last quarter, despite high initial engagement on social media ads."

Role 1

Lead Researcher

Ensures adherence to scientific steps.

Role 2

Data Analyst

Separates facts from assumptions.

Role 3

Question Designer

Narrows broad topic into focused question.

Role 4

Spokesperson

Prepares presentation for Plenary.

Team Deliverable: Scientific Refinement Matrix

Step 1: Define Broad Topic Example: "Why customers leave." (Too broad!)
Step 2: Identify Specific Psychological Variables Consider variables: Customer satisfaction, brand loyalty, unboxing experience, price perception, cognitive dissonance.
Step 3: Formulate Focused Research Question "How does package delivery time affect post-purchase customer satisfaction in buyers aged 18-24?"
